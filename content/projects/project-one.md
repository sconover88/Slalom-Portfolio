## Discovery & Research

We began with a thorough discovery phase to ensure we were solving the right problems before designing anything.

- **Stakeholder Interviews** — We conducted in-depth interviews with business stakeholders to understand the goals, constraints, and vision for the platform. These conversations helped us align on scope and surface business needs that weren't immediately obvious.
- **User Interviews** — We interviewed account executives and CSPs to understand their day-to-day workflows, pain points, and mental models. These sessions gave us a deep understanding of how they used each of the six legacy applications, how they interacted with clients, and where the biggest frustrations lived.
- **Service Design Blueprints & Journey Mapping** — We mapped the end-to-end experience across both the user and business perspectives, making the complexity of the existing process visible and identifying key opportunities for improvement.

Research findings were synthesized and presented back to stakeholders in dedicated research decks. We also had the privilege of presenting high-level updates directly to the **CEO on a monthly basis**, ensuring alignment at the highest level throughout the project.

Throughout the entire engagement, we maintained a cadence of **weekly cross-functional meetings** with stakeholders, product owners, scrum masters, project managers, and developers — using these touchpoints for design reviews, feasibility discussions, concept approvals, and ongoing alignment.

## Process

### Phase 1 — Feature Parity & Foundation

With a clear picture of the existing landscape, we began designing the platform one section at a time. The two Slalom designers each owned different areas of the application, while the client-side designer focused on additional research guides, user interview questions, and stakeholder facilitation.

Phase 1 had a focused goal: **ensure that everything a user could do across the six legacy tools could be done within this single application.** The platform was designed to guide CSPs through a structured, step-by-step proposal creation flow:

1. **Proposal Setup** — Capturing the client's advertising goals, including ad type (linear TV, streaming, search, social), target audience (age, gender, income, market intent, and more), geography (from individual households to national campaigns), event targeting, budget, and flight dates.
2. **Review** — A comprehensive summary of all entered information before moving forward.
3. **Proposal Output** — A clean, structured proposal ready for review and delivery.

Once designs were approved, Phase 1 was launched to a **pilot group of 15 users**. To gather real-world feedback, we conducted a **3-month diary study** with approximately 15 active participants — capturing how users interacted with the application in their actual work environment, surfacing usability issues, and validating our design decisions.

### Phase 2 — AI-Powered Recommendations

With Phase 1 validated, we moved into the most transformative part of the project: **integrating AI-driven proposal recommendations.**

While the development and data engineering teams built the underlying AI model — trained on historical proposal data — our team designed the experience around it. The goal was to make the recommendations feel helpful and empowering, not prescriptive or intimidating.

Key design decisions included:

- **Side-by-Side Proposal Comparison** — Users were presented with their original proposal alongside an AI-recommended version, with clear callouts explaining *what* was changed and *why* (e.g., budget reallocation, audience broadening, event additions).
- **Flexible Acceptance** — Users could accept the recommendation as-is, stick with their original, or edit the recommended proposal to find a middle ground — keeping the client's preferences while still benefiting from data-driven guidance.
- **Product Recommendations** — In addition to optimizing existing selections, the AI surfaced additional advertising products the client could stretch into to increase reach and ad performance.
- **Real-Time AI Feedback** — As users adjusted individual proposal elements, the AI dynamically recalculated and updated figures in real time, allowing reps to quickly iterate and fine-tune proposals with immediate feedback.
- **Personalizations** — A dedicated step allowed users to include or exclude specific channels or websites, giving them granular control over the final proposal.
- **Presentation Generation** — The platform automatically generated a polished, client-facing digital presentation. Users could also download standardized assets for use in their own presentations — directly addressing a stakeholder pain point around inconsistent client-facing materials.

![AI-powered side-by-side proposal comparison UI](proposal-platform/ai-comparison-ui.png)

Phase 2 launched to a **test group of 30 users**, eventually expanding to **10,000 users** ahead of a full rollout planned for **50,000 users**.

### Continued Engagement

Following the MVP launch, the client extended the engagement. The team continued to iterate on the platform — adding new features, refining the experience, and responding to user feedback. The relationship also expanded into a **second, ongoing project**, a testament to the trust built throughout the engagement.

## Design System

One of my most significant and sustained contributions to this project was the **creation and ownership of the platform's design system** — built entirely from scratch, with no existing component library as a foundation.

- **100+ custom components** designed and documented in Figma, covering everything from foundational UI elements to complex, interactive patterns specific to the proposal workflow.
- **Developer collaboration** — I worked closely with the development lead to ensure the Figma component library and the engineering component library stayed in sync, conducting regular reviews to validate that implemented components matched design specifications.
- **Team enablement** — I led multiple **Lunch & Learn sessions** and internal training workshops covering Figma best practices and how to effectively use and contribute to the design system, ensuring consistency across the team as the project scaled.
- **Ongoing maintenance** — As the product evolved, I continuously maintained and expanded the system to support new features and design patterns, keeping it a reliable source of truth for the entire team.
