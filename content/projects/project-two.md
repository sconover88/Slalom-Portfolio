## Discovery & Research

I joined the engagement a few weeks after the UX Strategist had begun, stepping in to support — and eventually lead — a rigorous discovery process.

- **User & Stakeholder Interviews** — I participated in and led interviews across **15 meetings** with stakeholders and end users, taking detailed notes and helping to guide conversations to surface the most critical insights.
- **Research Synthesis** — Using **Miro** and AI-assisted analysis, I synthesized findings from all 15 sessions to identify key themes, pain points, user needs, and business goals. This synthesis formed the foundation for every design decision that followed.
- **Research Presentation** — I created and co-presented a comprehensive **PowerPoint deck** to stakeholders, walking through research findings, insights, and recommendations. The presentation was designed to build alignment and buy-in before moving into design — and it did exactly that.

## Service Design Blueprint

With research complete, I began building the **Service Design Blueprint** — a structured artifact that mapped the end-to-end experience across users, advisors, business processes, and technology systems.

- I established the swim lanes and populated the blueprint with everything we had learned during discovery, creating a clear picture of the current state and what the future experience needed to support.
- As the project progressed, I facilitated **blueprint conversations with the technology team and multiple Subject Matter Experts (SMEs)** to validate assumptions, fill in gaps, and ensure the blueprint accurately reflected the full complexity of the system.
- The completed blueprint was delivered as a final artifact to the client, serving as a shared reference point for both the MVP and future state planning.

## Design System Migration

Before diving into MVP designs, I connected with the client's UX team and UX Director to understand what had already been done and identify how best to collaborate. The team had begun early-stage designs using **Material Design components**, with plans to migrate to **Salesforce's Lightning Design System**.

I stepped in to help lead and accelerate that migration:

- Worked directly with the UX team to implement the transition to the **Salesforce Lightning Design System**, ensuring components were applied correctly and consistently.
- Shared **best practices to avoid rework** — including guidance on how to structure designs and prototypes so that the migration wouldn't require rebuilding from scratch.
- Contributed to the migration of **300+ components**, helping the team establish a more scalable and maintainable design foundation going forward.

## Process

### MVP Design

With the design system in a strong place, I led the design of the **V1 MVP dashboard**. Throughout this process, I held regular meetings with stakeholders and the development team to align on feasibility, prioritize features by effort and business value, and deliver weekly project updates.

The MVP was focused and purposeful — delivering five core features that addressed the most critical user and business needs:

1. **Overview with Summary Metrics** — A clear, at-a-glance dashboard giving users an immediate snapshot of their financial picture.
2. **Account Balances** — A centralized view of all accounts, giving users visibility and control they had never had before.
3. **Advisor Communication** — A built-in channel for users to communicate directly with their financial advisor from within the platform, reducing reliance on email and phone.
4. **Authentication** — A secure, seamless login and identity verification experience.
5. **Action Center** — A curated hub of helpful articles, financial programs, and interactive calculators — empowering users to learn and take action on their own terms.

Throughout the MVP design process, I worked closely with stakeholders to refine and finalize the scope, ensuring every decision was grounded in both user needs and business priorities.

### Future State Vision

Once the MVP was finalized, I shifted focus to envisioning what the platform could become. Using **Figma Make** and **Kiro**, I brought the future state to life through high-fidelity, interactive prototypes that gave stakeholders a tangible feel for the product's potential — something the client responded to enthusiastically.

The future state was shaped by a combination of the most frequently requested user needs, advisor and stakeholder input, and best practices from leading financial applications. Given the wide range of users — from those managing a single account to those with complex, multi-account portfolios — the future state was designed to scale gracefully across all levels of financial sophistication.

**Future State Features:**

- **Investment & Portfolio Management** — Tools for users to track and manage their investments and overall portfolio performance.
- **Multi-Account Management** — A seamless experience for managing multiple accounts across different types and currencies.
- **Billing & Budgeting** — Budgeting tools and billing visibility to help users stay on top of their finances.
- **Debt Management** — Features to help users understand and manage outstanding debts.
- **Income & Expenses** — A clear breakdown of cash flow to support better financial decision-making.
- **Notification System** — Proactive, personalized alerts to keep users informed of important activity and opportunities.
- **Document Management** — A streamlined, in-platform document handling experience designed to replace the painful, email-heavy process that was one of the biggest complaints from both users and advisors. While it didn't make the MVP, it was prioritized as a fast follow given the severity of the pain point.
- **Currency & Exchanges** — Support for multi-currency accounts and exchange tracking.
- **Taxes** — Tools to help users understand and prepare for their tax obligations.
- **Asset Management** — A comprehensive view of user assets beyond traditional accounts.
- **Household Management** — The ability to manage finances at a household level, supporting families and joint account holders.
- **Financial Goals** — Goal-setting and tracking tools to help users work toward milestones like retirement, home ownership, or education funding.
- **Advisor Collaboration & Document Workflow** — A structured, in-platform process for working with advisors on paperwork-heavy tasks like opening new accounts and setting up 401(k)s — eliminating the back-and-forth emails and third-party troubleshooting that had frustrated users and advisors alike.

**AI Financial Assistant**

One of the most distinctive features of the future state was a contextual **AI Financial Assistant** — designed not as a traditional chatbot, but as an intelligent, unobtrusive helper inspired by the concept of Microsoft's Clippy, reimagined for the modern era.

Rather than forcing users to seek help, the assistant was context-aware — surfacing relevant guidance and recommendations based on where the user was in the app. Users had full control over how they engaged with it:

- **Guided Mode** — Users could opt to be fully guided through the app by the AI, ideal for less experienced users or those tackling complex tasks for the first time.
- **Passive Mode** — The assistant could sit quietly in the background, available when needed but never in the way — perfect for more experienced users who preferred independence.

The assistant could help users with account recommendations, opening new accounts, navigating features, and more. Critically, **advisors had full visibility into all AI interactions**, allowing them to review what their clients were asking and use those insights to provide more informed, proactive support. This feature was designed to bridge the gap between self-service empowerment and the trusted advisor relationship that was central to the company's identity.
